About

I am Weitong Li — Asa — a final-year B.S. in Artificial Intelligence student at Beijing Normal University-Hong Kong Baptist University (BNBU), with an exchange term at Hong Kong Baptist University. My work sits where methods research meets systems that must survive contact with the physical world.

Three threads run through my research. First, making models more trustworthy: my co-first-authored PRISM framework diagnoses and mitigates multi-dimensional LLM bias with no parameter updates, lifting Llama-2 on UNQOVER from 23.4% to 64.5%. Second, imaging for science and medicine: from domain-adaptive diffusion for microscopy deblurring to gravitational nested U-Nets for cardiac and abdominal segmentation. Third, AI that is embodied: vision-language models and PID-controlled thrusters aboard an autonomous underwater robot, and a humanoid skin-cancer screening system.

Most recently I worked on generative AI for drug discovery at the China Innovation & R&D Center of MSD (Merck) & Co., building multi-objective transfer-reinforcement-learning reward functions and AWS pipelines for reproducible multi-model experimentation.

I am currently looking for research internships and PhD positions for 2027 in multimodal and generative AI, scientific machine learning, and embodied intelligence.

Mar 2025 – Jun 2025

Principled Reasoning for Large-Scale Model Bias Identification & Mitigation

Published as co-first author at PRICAI 2025 (Springer LNCS)

  • Co-developed PRISM, a parameter-free framework for diagnosing and mitigating multi-dimensional LLM biases, unifying Dimensional Bias Identification (DBI), Targeted Example Synthesis (TES), and Discriminative Learning Enhancement (DLE) into a single inference-time pipeline.
  • Designed DBI and TES, combining causal-invariance testing, constrained bias-representation decomposition, and adversarial counterfactual synthesis to isolate interpretable spurious dimensions; empirical analysis identified an optimal representation dimensionality of k = 5.
  • Developed information-maximising contrastive example selection for 5-shot DLE prompting, improving Llama-2 on UNQOVER from 23.4% to 64.5% (+41.1 pts) and HANS by 14.8 pts; ablations identified DBI as the dominant component, with a 7.3-point average drop when removed.
